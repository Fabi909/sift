How Signal Works

Signal isn't sentiment, and it isn't a price prediction. It's a mechanical check on whether a coin's trading volume and news coverage actually back up what its price is doing — built from two independent checks, combined into one of three statuses. No hidden inputs, no manual overrides.

The Volume Check

Every coin's 24-hour trading volume is compared to its market cap. A big price move on trading volume that's a tiny fraction of the coin's size is easy to fake or coordinate; the same move on volume that's a meaningful share of the whole market cap is much harder to manufacture.

Under 2% — thin 2–8% — borderline 8%+ — strong

The News Check

Every coin's name and symbol are matched against roughly 15 tracked crypto news RSS sources, refreshed every 5 minutes. Multiple different outlets independently covering the same coin counts as stronger evidence than one single article does — the Signal tooltip's "News" line names the source(s) it actually found. When nothing matches, that's stated plainly as no matching news coverage, not folded silently into a generic "no evidence" read — the volume check above still runs and still means something on its own.

The Three Statuses

Validated Volume clears the 8% bar, AND real news coverage matches.
Mixed Either volume clears 8% with no matching news, or news matches while volume sits in the 2–8% borderline zone.
Unvalidated Volume is under the 2% thin-trading floor, whether or not news happens to mention the coin.

Signal Confidence (0–100)

A supplementary score for how strongly a coin clears its status — it never changes which of the three statuses a coin gets, only how convincingly it got there. Weighted 65% on volume strength (full marks at 16% of market cap traded, twice the "real activity" threshold above) and 35% on news corroboration (40 points per independent source, capped at 100 — so three or more outlets is effectively full marks). Deliberately kept out of the main table and shown only in the Signal tooltip and each coin's detail page, so the at-a-glance badge stays simple while the extra precision is there for anyone who wants it.

Signal Track Record

Every coin's Signal status gets snapshotted periodically. Later, each snapshot's price is compared against the coin's price now, bucketed by what the status actually said at snapshot time, across 3/7/14/30-day windows. That's the accountability check on the whole system: if Validated calls don't end up outperforming Unvalidated ones over time, the badge isn't earning its keep. Shown on the dashboard's Signal Track Record card (and per-coin on each coin's own detail page) once enough history has accumulated to make the comparison meaningful.

Source Reliability

The same accountability idea, grouped by news source instead of by Signal status. The first time an outlet's coverage matches a coin, that call and the coin's price at that moment get recorded; later, the average price change since each source's calls is computed across the same 3/7/14/30-day windows. A source only shows up once it has at least 3 tracked calls old enough for a given window — not enough history yet just means it isn't ranked, not that it's being counted against zero. Toggle "By Source" on the Track Record card to see it.

What Signal Isn't

Sift is a research tool, not financial advice, and it doesn't touch your wallet or execute trades — it only reads and structures public price and news data that already exists. A Validated status means the evidence backs up that something real is happening, not that the price will keep moving in any particular direction. Always do your own research before acting on anything you see here.
